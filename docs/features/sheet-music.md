# Sheet music cabinet and viewer

The cabinet right of the counter in the shop (`public/art/shop/cabinet.webp`). It holds music cylinders, each with a rolled-up piece of sheet music.

## For the player

The shop's cabinet holds the sheet music of the Muziekschatten collection, from [the back end](backend.md): fifteen pieces to a cabinet, each with its real scanned cover and no stars yet (they fill the shelves in order); the arrows walk along 65 cabinets. Their books open on the cover and a title page with all the makers, then a page saying the notes are still to come; they have no *Speel op de piano*. The piano's cabinet (*Kies muziek voor de piano*) still holds the songs below, which have notes.

1. **Cabinet** (*De bladmuziekkast*): clicking the cabinet in the shop opens its close-up full screen, `public/art/closeups/cabinet.webp`: three shelves of five compartments, each with a brass plaque. Each of the 15 [real songs](som-scores.md) stands in its own compartment as a sheet-music book: its printed cover, or a cover drawn for it (`js/ui/cover.js`). A composer's own piece (opened from the [portrait gallery](composers.md)) gets one of six cover pictures (`cover-1` … `cover-6`) with the title and the composer printed in the cartouche at the top. The plaque under it shows 1–3 stars. The shelves go by difficulty, as worked out by the import: top shelf ★, middle ★★, bottom ★★★, five each. Clicking a book lifts it out and opens the music.
   - **Two cabinets**, 20 pieces in all: *Liedjes uit de SOM-collectie* (the 15 songs) and *Muziek van de componisten* (the composers' 5 pieces). Brass arrows on the glass doors (the right one nudges to be noticed), ← / →, or a swipe across the cabinet go to the other one: the two cabinets slide sideways together in a short, smooth movement (0.42 s). A brass sign at the bottom names the cabinet, with a dot per cabinet. The cabinet in view is remembered for the next visit.
2. **Picking a book**: the chosen book comes out of its compartment, tilts a little and flies to the middle of the screen, growing as it comes closer. The desk fades in around it and the book lies there **closed**, with *Klik op het boek om het open te slaan* under it.
3. **The book** (*Bladmuziek*, full screen on a wooden desk): the player opens it. Pages turn **from right to left** around the spine, like a real book: first the cover (its inside is an old endpaper), which also slides the book over so the spine is in the middle, then the **title page**. The title page is always the same picture (`cover-inside.webp`); only the title, subtitle and composer printed on it change. Click the composer's name for their [biography](composers.md). Then come the music pages, two at a time: 4 lines of 2 bars each, big, with the title and composer at the top and the page number at the bottom.
   - **A real song's book is only the original**: the scan's pages, no endpaper and no caption. The first page lies on top: the printed cover, or for the album's tunes the album's cover. A song whose scan has no printed cover (e.g. *De tango*) is bound in the cover drawn for it in the cabinet, with blank paper on its back, so the book that flew from the cabinet is the one that opens (before, its cover vanished on the desk). When the next page is not the one printed on the back of the first (an album tune further in the album), that back is blank paper. Under the last page lies blank paper too.
   - **Real paper**: every page of every book bends while it turns (a cover is board: it bends only a little). Its edge lifts first and the part at the spine follows, it is lit while it lies flat and darker while it stands up, and it throws a shadow on the page it uncovers. A soft paper rustle plays with it. You can **drag a page** over with the mouse or a finger: it follows the pointer, and when you let go it finishes the turn if it got a third of the way (or was flicked), otherwise it falls back. Pointing at the outer edge of the right-hand page lifts its corner a little.
   - → / the right brass button / a click on the right-hand page turns forward; ← / the left button / a click on the left-hand page turns back (all the way to closing the book).
   - **Space** or *Speel op de piano* opens the piece on the [piano](piano.md). The book stays open underneath: *Terug* (or *Bladmuziek* on the piano) comes back to the same page. Opened from the piano's *Bladmuziek* button, *Speel op de piano* just goes back to the piano.
   - Every note has a coloured circle with its name under the staff. The colours are the same as on the piano keys.

When the cabinet is opened from the piano (*Kies bladmuziek*), picking a book loads the piece straight into the piano instead.

A real song's book is only the original: the pages of the scan, no title page and no engraved music (that is on the piano). The title page and engraved pages described above are for the composers' pieces.

The covers and the title page use **Playfair Display**, a high-contrast Didone typeface like the printed sheet music of around 1900, still easy to read (`--font-1900` in `css/style.css`).

## How it's built

- `js/ui/library.js`: `CABINETS` (name and pieces); each cabinet is a `.cab-page` carrying the cabinet picture (the stage's own picture is switched off), filled by `arrange()` (each piece on the shelf of its level, five to a shelf, else the nearest shelf with room). `go(d)` slides the two pages side by side with the Web Animations API (`translateX` only, 420 ms, ease-out), then hides the old one; `lastCabinet` is kept between visits. Uses `frame(…, { closeup })` (see [game shell](game-shell.md)) and places the books and plaques on the picture. `COLS` (plaque centres; the books sit `TUBE_DX` further right) and `SHELVES` (y of each shelf edge and plaque row) are measured in pixels of the 1672 × 941 picture and turned into percentages, so everything scales with it; sizes use `cqw` units of the picture box. Pieces go on the shelf of their `level`, at most five per shelf. The covers are `public/art/items/sheet-music/cover-1…6.webp`; `coverFor(piece)` picks one per piece (in turn) and `coverHtml()` builds the cover with its printed text, shared with the reader. The text box sits on the cartouche, in percentages of the cover, so it scales with it.
- `js/ui/library.js` → `flyToCentre()`: a copy of the clicked cover is put on top of everything (`.flying-book`, fixed position, in a box as wide as the cabinet picture so its `cqw` text sizes stay right) and moved with the Web Animations API to `closedCoverRect()` (where the closed book will lie, worked out from the window size the same way as the full-screen stage). Its text is set to the book's full composer name and size, so the swap is invisible. Then the book view opens with `noFade` (it fades in over the cabinet instead of through black; [game shell](game-shell.md)), and the copy fades away on top of the real book.
- `js/ui/sheet.js`: the book view, a full-screen view without a picture (`frame({ full: true })` without `closeup` gives a `.stage.desk`, a wooden desk drawn in CSS).
  - The book is two pages wide (`PAGE` gives the page size in `cqw`); closed (`.closed`) it is shifted left by a quarter so the cover is in the middle, open it slides back so the spine is (a transition on the whole book).
  - Every sheet is a `.leaf` on the right half with a `.front` (right-hand page) and a `.back` (the left-hand page after turning) face, `backface-visibility: hidden`. Leaf 0 is the cover with the endpaper on its back; the pages in reading order (title page, then the music) go on the fronts and backs of the next leaves. Under them, `.book-back` is the back cover with an endpaper. All music pages are engraved up front with `renderPage(host, parsed, i, 4, 2, 560)`: 4 lines of 2 bars on a narrow 560-unit staff, so the notes come out big on a portrait page.
  - The leaf hangs on the spine (`transform-origin: 0 50%`) inside a `perspective` box. `turned` counts the leaves on the left; each leaf gets a tiny `translateZ` depending on its side so the piles overlap in the right order; every page has a soft shadow along the spine.
  - Real songs (`.book.scanned`): the leaves are the scan pages (`.scan-page` figures, `object-fit: contain`; `.blank-page` for a blank back, inserted when `scans.pages[1]` isn't `scans.pages[0] + 1`), after a cover leaf with the drawn cover when `scans.cover` is false; the book's back is blank paper with the page edges as a shadow. Endpapers are only for a composer's piece.
  - **Bending pages** (all books, all leaves): `buildCurl(leaf)` gives a leaf its `.curl` the first time it is needed (the leaves that can turn next are prepared right after each turn): a copy of the leaf cut into `STRIPS` (18) nested `.strip`s. Each strip is hinged on the one before (`transform-origin: 0 50%`, `preserve-3d`) and shows its slice of the front page (`.sf`) and, turned around, of the back (`.sb`): a copy of the face's own HTML (scan, drawn cover, title page or engraved music) in a `.slice` `--pw` wide, shifted by `--col` slices, inside a strip that clips it. The cover leaf bends at 12% of `BEND`. While a leaf moves it gets `.curling`, its flat faces hide and the curl shows. `setCurl(leaf, t, dir)` sets every strip for `t` from 0 (flat on the right) to 1 (flat on the left): base angle `180 · ease(t)`, plus a bend of up to `BEND` (70°) times `sin(πt)` spread along the page (edge ahead, spine behind), clamped to 0–180°; each strip's `rotateY` is its angle minus the one before. `--sh` darkens a strip by `sin(angle)`, and `.cast` (the shadow on the page underneath, on the side the page is over) follows the turn. A key or button press runs `t` over `CURL_MS` (950 ms) with `requestAnimationFrame`. Dragging maps the pointer's travel (1.7 page widths = a whole turn) onto `t`, and on release `settle()` runs on to 1 or back to 0 from there. A peek runs to `t = 0.05`. When it is done, `turned` is updated and the flat leaf takes over in the same place. `rustle()` from [audio](audio.md) plays the paper sound.
  - Keyboard: ← → flip, Space → piano: `ui.open('piano', …)` on top of the book, so *Terug* comes back to it; with `fromPiano` (opened by the piano's *Bladmuziek*) it is `ui.close()`. The view exposes `pieceId` so the piano can tell whether the book below is its piece.
- `js/notation.js`: a small SVG engraver for single-line melodies in treble clef.
  - Staff with 10-unit line spacing, 4 bars per line; 2 lines per page in the reader, 1 in the piano.
  - Note heads (filled or open), stems up or down depending on the position, flags for eighths, sixteenths and thirty-seconds (all from the written value, `n.type`, so a triplet eighth looks like an eighth), dots, a small 3 over each triplet group, tie arcs, sharps/flats, ledger lines above and below, rests (glyphs from the *Noto Music* font), bar lines, a final double bar, time signature, bar numbers.
  - Bar widths depend on how many notes and how long they are.
  - Each note is a `<g class="note" data-i="…">` with a hidden highlight box; the piano adds `active` (lit up) and `done` (played) classes. `renderPage()` returns a map from note index to element.
  - Note names can be letters (C D E) or solfège (do re mi), see [piano](piano.md).

## Known limits / next steps

- Five compartments per shelf: a sixth piece of the same difficulty doesn't fit yet (it would need a second cabinet page or two cylinders per compartment).
- The Muziekschatten pieces have no notes and no level yet; see [the back end](backend.md).

- Melody only, treble clef only: no chords, no bass clef, no key signatures (accidentals are written on every note), no beams (each eighth has its own flag), no ties or lyrics.
- For real scores from Muziekschatten, an engraver like VexFlow or OpenSheetMusicDisplay (MusicXML) would be needed; the highlight hook (`data-i` per note) can stay the same.
